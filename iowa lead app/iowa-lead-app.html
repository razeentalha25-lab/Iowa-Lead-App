Suggested next steps

    Factors selected

    This educational tool cannot diagnose lead exposure or replace medical advice. A blood lead test, interpreted by a healthcare professional, is used to measure a person’s blood lead level.

    Nutrition can support health, but it cannot remove lead

    Regular meals with varied, nutritious foods are helpful for general health. Food or supplements do not prevent, diagnose, or treat lead exposure; discuss supplements with a healthcare professional.

    Calcium-containing foods

    Examples include milk, yogurt, cheese, calcium-set tofu, and some leafy greens.

    Iron-containing foods

    Examples include beans, lentils, fortified cereals, eggs, and lean meats.

    Vitamin C-containing foods

    Examples include oranges, berries, tomatoes, bell peppers, and broccoli.

    Do not delay testing or medical care in favor of dietary changes. Ask a clinician or registered dietitian about individual nutrition needs.

    Where to ask about testing

    Your healthcare provider Ask whether a blood lead test is appropriate, especially for young children or anyone with a possible exposure.
    Your local county public health department Ask about local lead testing, home assessment, and environmental health services in your area.
    Private well water testing Contact your local health department or an accredited drinking-water laboratory to ask how to collect a sample and test for lead.
